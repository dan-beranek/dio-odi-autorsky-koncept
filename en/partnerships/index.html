Six ways to collaborate

The scope should match the question the collaboration is meant to resolve.

The first step can be small. What matters is a clear subject, an expected output and a decision that both parties can make afterwards.

01

Expert dialogue

Critique of the concepts, model, segment or proposed operationalization. The output is a specific refinement, not a vague endorsement.

02

Pilot

A bounded intervention with a baseline, predefined changes, evaluation and the freedom to reach a null result.

03

Co-development

Translation of a validated problem into a repeatable process, data model, tool or integration.

04

Research partnership

Formalization of questions, variables and protocols, domain adaptation or a joint grant path.

05

Licensing

Defined use of know-how or methodology. Scope, territory, term and derivative rights are negotiated individually.

06

Strategic or capital agreement

Funding for one commercialization thesis, or a deliberate combination, with a concrete milestone and partner role.

Three investment theses

An investor funds a path to value—not a fourth track.

The site offers no valuation, financial forecast or implied product-market fit. Each thesis explicitly separates the present foundation, the development hypothesis and the nearest validation milestone.

Thesis 01

DIO for AI visibility

Current foundation: closest to the author’s practice and immediately deliverable as an expert intervention.

Hypothesis: paid cases will produce a repeatable methodology, vertical playbooks and a foundation for monitoring.

Milestone: an external pilot, implemented changes and documented utility for the partner.

Thesis 02

DIO for AI and entity data

Current foundation: a conceptual and operational model of entity reconstruction.

Hypothesis: co-development may lead to diagnostics, integration or another scalable technical product.

Milestone: a bounded data use case and a measurable change in system decisions.

Thesis 03

ODI

Current foundation: the theoretical layer, terminology, K6 and the publication corpus.

Hypothesis: vocabularies, ontologies, standardization or licensed know-how may acquire institutional value.

Milestone: an expert partner, a formalized output and a validated user of the knowledge layer.

First mover

Early participation brings influence—not automatic ownership.

A first partner creates value where its environment, data, critique or capital genuinely shapes the first repeatable application.

  • Direct access to the author and emerging know-how
  • An opportunity to shape the operational form of a selected track
  • Early experience with the methodology
  • An opportunity to co-create a domain application
  • Reference or founding status aligned with the actual contribution
  • An opportunity to negotiate deeper collaboration and broader rights

Principles of agreement

Clear rights before joint work begins.

Specific contractual terms are not universally fixed in advance. The default framework protects the author-led core while allowing rights to be negotiated in proportion to the partner’s actual contribution.

Default position

Authorship and foundational IP remain with the author

Unless a specific agreement states otherwise. A pilot does not automatically grant a license, equity or exclusivity.

Subject of negotiation

Broader rights can be negotiated specifically

According to scope, risk, data, technical work, capital, distribution and the parties’ other contributions.

A qualified proposal

Five answers that make a concrete counterproposal possible.

No long pitch deck is required. The first message should show a genuine fit between a DIO/ODI track and what the partner is addressing, owns or can make available.

Which track?

DIO for AI visibility, DIO for AI and entity data, ODI—or a precisely described intersection.

What problem?

A specific entity, decision, research question, market opportunity or institutional need.

What will the partner contribute?

Data, a case, technical capacity, expertise, market access, institutional support or capital.

What does the partner seek?

A pilot output, methodology, technical integration, research result, license, position or equity.

Under what conditions?

Scope, timeline, decision milestone and any expectations concerning rights or exclusivity.
