03 · ODI for knowledge infrastructure

Digital identity as an object of inquiry.

Ontology of Digital Identity is a distinct theoretical, terminological, research and IP layer. It examines the conditions under which digital identity becomes recognizable, stable, relationally grounded, interpretable and reconstructable.

Who it is for

AI R&D, research teams, ontology and knowledge-graph designers, knowledge producers, data brokers, and standards or regulatory environments.

Research field ↓

A distinct layer

ODI is not a footnote to DIO.

DIO engages a specific identity with an optimization objective. ODI examines the conditions under which digital identity exists, can be known and can break down—without requiring immediate application.

The two layers share concepts and the K6 model, but answer different questions. DIO asks how to intervene. ODI asks what must first be defined before identity, intervention and the resulting reconstruction can be discussed coherently.

Epistemic restraint

Not “objective truth,” but reliable and auditable reconstruction.

ODI treats digital identity as a distributed, emergent object. Different observers work from different traces and rules; the goal is therefore not a metaphysically final description, but transparent conditions for compatible interpretations.

Integrity

Semantic integrity

Claims, relationships and representations do not undermine one another without a visible explanation.

Origin

Provenance

It is possible to trace who created a claim, what supports it and the context in which it applies.

Uncertainty

Confidence

The distinction among data, interpretation and inference remains visible.

Alignment

Identification consistency

Different systems can arrive at compatible definitions of an entity and its relationships.

K6 as a shared language

Six dimensions of identity’s relational field.

The dimensions do not form a sequence. Any change in representation can alter interpretation, trust, relationships and the resulting reconstruction—while also redefining how the entity itself is bounded.

01

Entity

What is stabilized as a distinguishable interpretive node, and where its boundaries lie.

02

Representation

The traces, signs, data and statements through which the entity becomes present to each observer.

03

Interpretation

The meaning produced from representations within a specific mode of reading and decision-making.

04

Trust

How reliability, authority, evidential support and resilience to contradiction are established.

05

Relationship

How connections to other entities, works, roles and sources anchor meaning.

06

Reconstruction

How the whole is reassembled from available traces, and with what loss or uncertainty.

K6 is the operational and conceptual framework of DIO/ODI. Its universal empirical validity remains open to external evaluation.

Research agenda

Questions that can be refined, operationalized and falsified.

ODI gains value not by expanding abstraction, but by translating concepts into research problems, metrics, domain adaptations and open expert critique.

Identity

Under what conditions do distributed traces stabilize as one entity—and when do they split into multiple nodes?

Modes of reading

When are human, search, graph and model-based reconstructions compatible—and when are they not?

Time

How can an identity update be distinguished from a discontinuity, conflation or illegitimate overwrite?

Evidence

How should provenance and confidence affect the weight of a claim, relationship or derived interpretation?

Intervention

How can we tell that a change in representation improved reconstruction rather than merely hiding an inconvenient contradiction?

What ODI could create

Knowledge infrastructure for working with digital identity.

The following outputs are directions for co-development. Their form and associated rights must arise from a specific partnership, not from a universal license promised in advance.

Terminology

Vocabulary and conceptual relationships

Definitions, boundaries and connections among identity, representation, interpretation, trust, relationship and reconstruction.

Formalization

Ontologies and data models

Translation of selected concepts into a machine-readable structure for a specific domain.

Evaluation

Metrics and protocols

Methods for measuring coherence, distinguishability, provenance and reconstructability.

Research

Interdisciplinary projects

Connections among information science, semiotics, psychology, data science and AI studies.

Adaptation

Domain applications

Precise adaptation of concepts to public administration, research, media, enterprise data or another environment.

IP

Licensing and standardization

Use of defined know-how in tools, methodologies, standards or knowledge products.

A suitable partnership

Critical, interdisciplinary and specifically bounded.

ODI is not looking merely for agreement. It seeks environments where its concepts can be refined, challenged and translated into usable structures.

  • Critical expert review
  • Formalization and operationalization of concepts
  • Research and grant partnerships
  • Domain adaptation
  • Licensing or standards collaboration

ODI for knowledge infrastructure
